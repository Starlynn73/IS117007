ON THE GO / RESEARCH NOTEBOOK

Three retailers.
Three value stories.

A focused comparison of communication and shopping choices, with the same homepage → product → bag → checkout-entry lens.

SCOPE & METHOD

Comparable journeys.
Different garments.

The original November 9 session sampled one available white tee per retailer, quantity one, plus the homepage, product page, bag and checkout entry. UNIQLO and H&M were sampled in men’s categories; Everlane in women’s. Fit, cut, size and price are not equivalent. This is a user-experience comparison, not a quality ranking or market census.

The studies retain the original records and screenshots. They are historical session evidence, not a claim that today’s live prices or pages are identical. No contact or payment data was entered and no orders were placed.

02 / THE THREE LENSES

Look closely before applying a label.

DESIGN

Function & expression

Repeated grids, hierarchy and predictable controls can echo modernist function. Collage and mixed visual languages can invite a postmodern reading. Neither bright colors nor a large photograph alone establishes a movement.

BRAND CHARACTER

An interpretation, not a diagnosis

Everyperson suggests approachable belonging; Sage, informed understanding; Lover, aesthetic desire; Caregiver, concern for others. These describe our reading of communication—not official strategy or shoppers’ personalities.

PERSUASION

Name the mechanism

Reviews can signal social proof; credible expertise can support authority; real time limits can support scarcity; styled people can invite liking. We have not measured whether any cue changes behavior.

Framework sources: Pearson, Influence at Work and the V&A. Ordinary discounts are not automatically reciprocity; required size selection is not a voluntary commitment establishing consistency.

03 / CROSS-SITE SYNTHESIS

What the evidence changes for On the Go.

Observed pattern Inference Project implication What still needs checking
All three pair shirts with styled people or outfits. Outfit imagery is already competitive territory. Give each look a short, situation-specific recipe. Can a peer adapt it without buying the full outfit?
UNIQLO and Everlane provide supporting product information. An expressive hero cannot replace purchase clarity. Keep size, price, demo status and unknown facts easy to find. Can a peer explain what is known before adding to bag?
H&M’s bag adds $6.99 estimated shipping to a $7.99 tee. A low product price can obscure the comparison. Label demo costs and show a complete calculation. Do totals update correctly when quantity changes?
Everlane exposes a paid-protection choice in the bag. Optional extras can add uncertainty even in a restrained interface. Avoid surprise extras or preselected add-ons. Can a visitor finish the demo without an unexpected charge?
UNIQLO offers guest checkout; H&M stops at email entry. Contact requirements and account choices affect the path. Keep the planned demo path explicit and concise. Can a peer complete it without coaching?

04 / OTHER ALTERNATIVES

The competitor may already be in the drawer.

A student might restyle a tee they own, buy a multipack, choose secondhand, or make no purchase. These are substitutes to investigate, not measured preferences. Free styling should remain useful even if the visitor never buys our demo product.

Research conclusion

Keep the existing styling-focused direction, but test its usefulness through achievable outfit recipes. The research does not establish an unmet market, a superior garment, a cost advantage or increased confidence.
