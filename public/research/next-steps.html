01 / EVIDENCE TO FEATURES

Keep every proposal connected to a finding.

Finding Proposed story Essential feature Observable check
Styled photos are common; useful differentiation is uncertain. As a student meeting friends, I want a look I can adapt using what I own. One short outfit recipe with an accessory/sneaker pairing and substitution. After viewing, a peer can name one pairing and one usable substitution without coaching.
Supporting product information accompanies the imagery. As a visitor, I want to know what is confirmed before choosing a size. Clearly labeled sizes, fit-information limits and demo product facts. The peer finds size guidance; missing size blocks adding and gives a helpful message.
H&M’s bag changes the cost picture. As a demo shopper, I want an understandable order total. Quantity controls and an itemized, clearly fictional cost summary. Quantity 2 produces twice the unit price, with every demo charge labeled and the total recalculated.
Optional extras and contact gates add decisions. As a demo shopper, I want a clear path with control over optional choices. Short labeled synthetic-data checkout, input checks and confirmation. Missing required input shows an error; valid synthetic input reaches confirmation without unexpected extras.
Final store requires actual SQLite persistence. As the demonstrator, I want to retrieve a saved synthetic order. Runnable backend, SQLite order record and documented start command. Place a synthetic order, inspect its reference, restart and show that the same record remains.

02 / TWO APPROACHES TO COMPARE

Hold the shirt and promise steady.

PROPOSED A

Outfit first

Lead with a complete look and a short recipe; place the tee’s purchase controls after the help. Test whether the guidance is understood before shopping.

PROPOSED B

Product first, help alongside

Lead with tee selection and product information; put the same outfit recipe beside the product. Test whether useful styling can remain discoverable without delaying the purchase task.

Use the same product, wording, facts and outfit content so the comparison isolates organization. These are AI-drafted proposals for the learner’s Plan decision; no preference or test result is claimed.

03 / A REAL PEER WALKTHROUGH

Ask for an action.
Record what happens.

  1. Ask the peer to imagine getting ready to meet friends and adapt one outfit using something they already own.
  2. Let them explore without explaining where to click. Record what they find, misunderstand or skip.
  3. Ask what the offer is, who it serves, why they would trust it and what they would do next.
  4. Give the same task in both working versions once built; record observations before asking for a preference.
  5. Link a specific issue, respond, make a meaningful change and repeat the task.

This walkthrough is proposed. No peer research feedback, interview or final-store retest has been recorded. A useful classroom observation is not market validation.
