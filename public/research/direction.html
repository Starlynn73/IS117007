ON THE GO / RESEARCH NOTEBOOK

A useful look.
A specific situation.

Keep the learner’s existing On the Go direction, compare the alternative honestly, and make the remaining assumptions visible.

01 / POSITIONING

Two ways to give the same tee meaning.

Question A / Affordable everyday essential B / Styling help & confidence
Proposed value A useful everyday basic at a suitable total price. A white tee shown in ready-to-copy looks for college students.
Buyer’s question Is this basic worth its total cost? Can I adapt this look for meeting friends?
Competitive pressure Established low-price basics and familiar retailers. All three sampled retailers already show styled models or outfits.
Evidence gap No verified sourcing cost, price advantage or quality claim. No observed evidence yet that our guidance is easier to use.
Project decision Alternative considered; not selected. Existing learner-selected position; usefulness remains to be tested.

02 / TWO PERSONA HYPOTHESES

Same audience.
Different getting-ready moments.

PRIMARY / MEETING FRIENDS

“How could I put this together?”

Situation: a college student preparing for a casual hangout.
Goal: a look that feels cool and confident.
Uncertainty: a plain tee can feel basic; what should go with it?
Page task: find a complete look and a pairing move they can copy or adapt.
Evidence: learner-selected situation and prior Brand Studio concern; customer need unvalidated.

SECONDARY / GOING TO CLASS

“What is easy to wear today?”

Situation: a student getting dressed for class.
Goal: a simple everyday outfit with little effort.
Uncertainty: inspiration needs to be quick and practical.
Page task: understand one combination without a long shopping journey.
Evidence: AI-drafted contrast reused from Brand Studio; details remain assumptions.

03 / CHOSEN DIRECTION

College students, led by meeting friends.

The existing research record preserves the learner’s explanation: students should get styling options they can copy or adapt. Seeing the tee worn in a cool, confident look may help them imagine recreating it. This explains the project choice; it does not demonstrate a customer preference or purchase effect.

Source → inference → decision

The three retailers show that styling is established. The useful next experiment is therefore concise, achievable instructions for a specific student situation—not a claim that outfit photography itself is unique.

05 / HONEST PERSUASION

Give the visitor something useful.

RECIPROCITY

Free outfit help

Existing outfit ideas can be read without signup or purchase. The proposed benefit is useful guidance before asking for a sale; no conversion result has been measured.

CONSISTENCY

An optional plan

The existing Save a look action opens a matching checklist. It is voluntary, removable and separate from the demo bag. Required size selection would not establish this principle.

LIKING / HYPOTHESIS

A look worth adapting

Confident styling may help a visitor imagine wearing the outfit. This follows the learner’s stated rationale; it is not evidence that the audience will buy.

06 / WHAT COULD CHANGE THE DIRECTION

Let the next observation matter.

  • If peers see only a photo and cannot explain a pairing, add a short recipe before adding more imagery.
  • If the look feels too expensive or difficult, offer substitutions using owned items; do not invent a budget claim.
  • If peers understand the recipe but have no reason to buy, keep the guidance useful and avoid claiming demand.
  • If product uncertainty blocks the task, improve facts and labels rather than adding pressure.
